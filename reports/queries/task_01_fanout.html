1. Analytical Significance & What It Means

Scenario & Frontier Trap
Business Scenario & Problem

Demonstrates the Chasm Trap where joining multiple 1-to-many child tables (discounts and shipments) to an orders table causes row multiplication before aggregation, inflating financial sums.

⚡ The Frontier Trap (Why LLMs Fail)

Joining multiple 1:N child tables (order_discounts has 2 rows, order_shipments has 2 rows) directly to orders before GROUP BY creates an intermediate Cartesian fan-out of 2 * 2 = 4 rows per order. Aggregating over these inflated rows multiplies base_amount by 4x ($100.00 -> $400.00), total discounts by 2x ($15.00 -> $30.00), and shipping by 2x ($10.00 -> $20.00). Proper solution requires pre-aggregating child tables to 1 row per order in CTEs or correlated subqueries before joining.

Prompt / User Request Given to Model
Calculate total gross sales (sum of base_amount), total discount amount, and total shipping fees for each merchant. Ensure multiple discount line items and multiple shipment records for the same order do NOT multiply or artificially inflate the totals.

Required output columns:
- merchant_id (INT)
- merchant_name (VARCHAR)
- total_gross_sales (DECIMAL)
- total_discount_amount (DECIMAL)
- total_shipping_fees (DECIMAL)

Row order does not matter.

4. Queries Executed (Candidate vs Golden Ground Truth)

Beautified & Syntax Highlighted
Database Engine Context: This query was executed and profiled on DuckDB 2.0 In-Memory Engine with equivalent analytical syntax and execution verification on PostgreSQL 18.7 + Extensions (Docker Compose).
Candidate SQL Executed by gemini-3.8-flash-low (Runtime on 1M Rows: 88.52 ms) 
WITH order_level_discounts AS
  (SELECT order_id,
          sum(amount) AS discount_amount
   FROM order_discounts
   GROUP BY order_id),
     order_level_shipments AS
  (SELECT order_id,
          sum(shipping_fee) AS shipping_fee
   FROM order_shipments
   GROUP BY order_id),
     merchant_order_metrics AS
  (SELECT o.merchant_id,
          sum(o.base_amount) AS total_gross_sales,
          coalesce(sum(d.discount_amount), 0.00) AS total_discount_amount,
          coalesce(sum(s.shipping_fee), 0.00) AS total_shipping_fees
   FROM orders o
   LEFT JOIN order_level_discounts d ON o.order_id = d.order_id
   LEFT JOIN order_level_shipments s ON o.order_id = s.order_id
   GROUP BY o.merchant_id)
SELECT m.merchant_id,
       m.merchant_name,
       coalesce(mom.total_gross_sales, 0.00) AS total_gross_sales,
       coalesce(mom.total_discount_amount, 0.00) AS total_discount_amount,
       coalesce(mom.total_shipping_fees, 0.00) AS total_shipping_fees
FROM merchants m
LEFT JOIN merchant_order_metrics mom ON m.merchant_id = mom.merchant_id;
Golden Reference Ground Truth SQL (Runtime on 1M Rows: 6.05 ms) 
WITH agg_discounts AS
  (SELECT order_id,
          sum(amount) AS total_discount
   FROM order_discounts
   GROUP BY order_id),
     agg_shipments AS
  (SELECT order_id,
          sum(shipping_fee) AS total_shipping
   FROM order_shipments
   GROUP BY order_id)
SELECT m.merchant_id,
       m.merchant_name,
       sum(o.base_amount) AS total_gross_sales,
       coalesce(sum(d.total_discount), 0.00) AS total_discount_amount,
       coalesce(sum(s.total_shipping), 0.00) AS total_shipping_fees
FROM merchants m
JOIN orders o ON m.merchant_id = o.merchant_id
LEFT JOIN agg_discounts d ON o.order_id = d.order_id
LEFT JOIN agg_shipments s ON o.order_id = s.order_id
GROUP BY m.merchant_id,
         m.merchant_name;
